Privacy Policy

Effective 5 September 2026 · Applies to 1m for iPhone (com.mobileproblemsolver.app), version 1.0 onward.

The short version

  • We collect nothing. The app has no server, no accounts, no analytics and no third-party SDKs. Nothing about you or your material is sent to us, because there is nowhere for it to be sent.
  • Your material stays on your iPhone until you press Share yourself and choose where it goes.
  • When you share, you are handing your content to another app you picked. From that moment that app's privacy policy applies, not this one.
  • Redaction is a help, not a guarantee. Check what you are sending before you send it.

1m captures logs, code or an error on your screen, prepares a prompt around it, and hands the whole package to an app you already have through the iOS share sheet. This policy explains exactly what happens to that material at every step.

1. Who this policy is from

1m is developed and published by Oleksandr Miahkyi, an independent developer. Throughout this policy, “we” means that developer and “the app” means 1m for iPhone. You can reach us at asoft.general.support@gmail.com.

2. What we collect: nothing

The app does not collect, transmit, store or process any personal data on our behalf. This is a technical fact about how it is built rather than a promise about how we behave:

  • There is no server belonging to this app, and no backend of any kind.
  • The app contains no networking code of its own — no HTTP client, no web views, and not one web address anywhere in its source. It never opens a connection to us or to any third party. The only network activity is iOS acting on the app's behalf, and only ever to Apple: checking your purchase or trial with the App Store, and the two features described further down. None of it reaches a server of ours, because there is none.
  • There are no accounts. You never register, sign in, or give us an email address.
  • There is no analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or advertising, and no third-party software development kits are linked into the app at all.
  • There is no tracking as the App Store defines it. The app does not use the Advertising Identifier, does not present the App Tracking Transparency prompt, and does not link anything about you to data from other companies.

The app ships with an Apple privacy manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) that declares no tracking and no collected data types. Its App Store privacy label reads Data Not Collected. Those two declarations and this page are intended to say the same thing; if you ever find that they do not, please tell us and we will correct it.

3. What is stored on your device

Everything the app keeps stays inside its own sandboxed container on your iPhone, where no other app can read it.

History stores the redacted version, never the original. If you chose to hide something before sharing, the copy kept on your device is the one with it already covered — painted out of the image, the PDF page or the video frame, and replaced with <sensitive> in your typed text. What you asked to hide is not written to long-term storage in the clear.

Deleting the app removes everything above — with four deliberate exceptions. iOS deletes the app's entire container, so your requests, attachments, prompts and settings go with it. Four small items in the iOS Keychain survive on purpose, because they are what stops reinstalling the app from silently restarting a finished free trial:

  • the date this app first ran on this iPhone, which is when your free trial began;
  • the date this Apple Account first downloaded the app, as reported by the App Store;
  • a yes/no flag recording that a valid access code was entered;
  • the most recent date the app has seen, which is what makes setting your device clock backwards ineffective.

None of the four contains personal data or identifies you, none is transmitted to us or to anyone else, and none is synchronised off your device. Three dates and a yes/no flag is the whole of it. You can also clear History at any time from Settings inside the app.

4. What happens when you share — read this one

The whole purpose of 1m is to hand your prepared request to another app. This is the only route by which anything leaves your device, and it never happens on its own:

  • Nothing is shared until you press Share and then pick a destination in the standard iOS share sheet.
  • The app cannot send anything automatically, silently, or in the background. It has no ability to choose a destination for you.
  • Once you pick a destination, that app receives your content and its own privacy policy and terms govern what happens next. If the destination is a chat assistant, your material may be transmitted to and processed on that company's servers, and may be retained or used according to their policy and your settings with them.

We have no visibility into, control over, or relationship with whatever app you choose. We are not affiliated with the makers of any destination app. Please read the privacy policy of the app you are sharing into — that is where your content actually goes.

5. Purchases, the free trial and access codes

1m offers a free trial and a one-time paid unlock. Deciding which applies means asking Apple a question, so it is worth being exact about what that involves.

The free trial runs for three days from the first time you open the app on this iPhone. It does not need an internet connection to begin, and it does not restart if you delete the app and install it again. If more than thirty days have passed since this Apple Account downloaded the app, the trial has already closed by the time you first open it; that limit is the only thing preventing the trial from being restarted indefinitely. When the trial ends the app keeps working with a reduced set of features — nothing you have already prepared is removed or made unreadable.

  • Apple processes any purchase — we do not. Payment is handled entirely by the App Store under your Apple Account. We never see, receive or store your payment method, billing address, Apple Account email, or any other identifier. Apple does not give them to us.
  • The app asks the App Store what you already own, and when this Apple Account first downloaded the app. That request is made by StoreKit, Apple's own framework, and is answered by Apple. It tells Apple nothing Apple does not already know, and the answer goes nowhere but your device.
  • The answer is cached on your device, so the question is asked once. After that the app works offline indefinitely. It never asks you for your App Store password in order to re-check.
  • The unlock is a single purchase, tied to your Apple Account. It is not a subscription: there is nothing to renew and nothing to cancel. Because it belongs to your Apple Account rather than to this iPhone, it works on any device you sign in to, Restore purchases in Settings brings it back after reinstalling, and — if you turn on Family Sharing — your family members can use it. The app learns of a shared purchase in exactly the same way it learns of your own: by asking the App Store what this Apple Account is entitled to. It is never told who else is in your family, or anything about them.
  • Refunds are requested from Apple, not from us. The button in Settings opens Apple's own refund sheet; the request goes to Apple, who decide. We have no role in approving or refusing one, and we are not told the reason you gave. If a refund is granted, the app simply stops seeing the purchase and returns to its reduced set of features.
  • Access codes are never stored and never transmitted. If you enter one, it is hashed on your device (SHA-256, using Apple's CryptoKit) and compared against hashes built into the app. The code you typed is not saved, not sent anywhere, and cannot be recovered from what is kept — which is only a yes/no flag saying that a valid code was entered.

We receive no report of who bought the app, who is in a trial, or who redeemed a code. Apple gives us the same aggregated sales figures it gives every developer; that is Apple's reporting to us, not data this app collects from you.

6. Apple services the app uses on your behalf

The app is built entirely on Apple frameworks. Two of them can contact Apple's servers, and neither sends anything to us:

Dictation (Speech framework)

Used only while you hold the microphone button to dictate. The app requests on-device recognition whenever your language supports it, specifically so that what you dictate — often a production incident — does not leave your phone if it does not have to. Where your language has no on-device model available, iOS falls back to server-based recognition, exactly as the system keyboard's own dictation does, and the audio is processed by Apple. This is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy. The app requests microphone and speech recognition permission before this can happen.

Translating your prompt (Translation framework)

Used only when you tap Translate on your own typed text. iOS may download language assets from Apple to make a language pair available. The translation itself is performed by the system. Your text is not sent to us at any point.

Checking your purchase or trial (StoreKit)

Described in section 5 above. iOS contacts the App Store to confirm what this Apple Account owns; the result is cached on your device so it happens once rather than on every launch.

The app uses no other network-capable service. Text recognition, sensitive-data detection, redaction and everything else run entirely on your device.

7. On-device processing

These features never transmit anything anywhere:

  • Reading textApple's Vision framework reads text out of images, PDF pages and video frames on the device.
  • Finding sensitive dataPattern rules and Apple's NaturalLanguage framework look for secrets, emails, hosts, IP addresses, paths, card numbers and names.
  • RedactionAnything you leave switched on is painted into the image, PDF or video, and replaced with a marker in your text, before the file is shared.

8. What redaction can and cannot promise

The app tries to find sensitive material and cover it before you share. It is not exact, and it is not a guarantee. In the app's own words:

Neither reading the text nor finding what is sensitive in it is exact. Text can be missed on a blurry or angled picture, a secret in an unusual shape may not be recognised as one, and something harmless can be covered instead — so full removal is not guaranteed. Check every attachment and everything you have typed yourself before you share, and treat what this pass found as a help rather than as a promise.

Redaction can only cover what the app was able to read. Anything it could not open or read is reported to you as not checked rather than as clean. Archives are refused as attachments outright, because nothing in the app can read inside one. You remain responsible for what you choose to send.

11. Your rights

Regulations such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights of access, correction, deletion and portability over personal data a company holds about you. We hold none. There is no account to close, no record to request and nothing for us to delete, because nothing ever reaches us. Data held on your own device is under your control: clear History in the app's Settings, or delete the app to remove everything at once.

For material you have already shared into another app, those rights are exercised with that app's provider.
